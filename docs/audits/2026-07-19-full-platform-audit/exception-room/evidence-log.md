## Browser-confirmed Exception Room findings

### EXC-BP1-01: Shared chrome clips the flagship game on its primary viewport

At 390 x 844, shared Product Lab chrome consumes the first 114 pixels, then `.exception-shell` still claims 844 pixels of height. The document itself reports only 844 pixels of scroll height, so the lower 114 pixels cannot be reached. At desktop, additional world padding narrows the intended 390 pixel shell to about 342 pixels. This is not a small spacing defect. It breaks the full-screen mobile canvas contract.

### EXC-BP1-02: The game awards Balanced Operator for zero evidence review

All 12 cases were completed through the browser without opening a single evidence item. The resulting profile was Balanced Operator with Safety 100, Service 100, Capacity 96, 12 safe completions, zero breaches, and 200 XP. Evidence inspected 0 percent appears only as a subordinate detail row. The result tells a portfolio reviewer that the product rewards correct guesses more strongly than accountable oversight.

### EXC-BP1-03: Deadline guidance is factually wrong in the visible queue

For audited seed 6240 at tick 0, the three available cases were due in two, three, and two ticks. The visible header said 1 case due next tick. The component renders that phrase for every positive remaining value and hardcodes the count as one. A product about queue judgment cannot ship inaccurate priority information.

### EXC-BP1-04: Evidence scoring measures interaction, not review quality

A second full browser campaign opened exactly one evidence item on every case. The debrief reported Evidence inspected 100 percent, even though the opened item did not have to be required, conflicting, or decisive. Combined with the zero-evidence perfect-safety path, this score cannot support the product's oversight claim.

### EXC-BP2-01: The core review loop loses keyboard context

Keyboard activation works, but opening Correct or Escalate, returning from a picker, revealing an outcome, and continuing to a new case all leave focus on Body. The replacement content is not announced through a deliberate live region. A keyboard or screen-reader user must rediscover the interface after the most important state changes.

### EXC-BP2-02: Evidence is visually subordinated

The case summary is hidden, evidence summaries are tiny and clamped, and the player can submit with 0 of 2 reviewed. The screen therefore makes AI recommendation and action buttons easier to consume than the facts needed to challenge the model.

### EXC-BP2-03: Preferred and acceptable outcomes look identical

The browser screenshots for a preferred resolution and an acceptable resolution both show Sound judgment with the same check icon, color, hierarchy, and CTA. The engine distinguishes these outcomes, but the product does not teach the difference.

## Strengths to preserve

- The entry communicates the strongest portfolio story in Product Lab: model recommendation, human judgment, finite capacity, operational stakes, and synthetic data.
- The dark neon visual system feels distinct, authored, and substantially more game-like than generic product UI.
- Queue severity, case selection, AI recommendation, evidence groups, capacity, and three decision types create a credible operational simulation surface.
- Unsafe and unnecessary outcomes use appropriately different tone and explain what the decision protected or cost.
- All 12 cases, all three shifts, run-new-queue, back-to-briefing, and XP persistence work without console or page errors.
- Internal scrolling keeps the selected-case actions reachable at narrow widths, even though the full composition does not reflow cleanly.

## Evidence limits

- The engine error surface could not be reached through a supported browser interaction. Its presentation remains source-reviewed, not visually certified.
- Rapid duplicate input did not reproduce the source-level race in this Chromium run. It should remain covered by an automated regression test.
- Audio was verified through browser API instrumentation, not physical speakers.
- Reveal and debrief clipping did not reproduce with the current English copy at 390 x 844. The CSS inconsistency remains a regression risk rather than a browser-confirmed defect.
